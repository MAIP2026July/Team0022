IdeaHub AI - AI Collaboration Log
Product: IdeaHub AI
Team: Team0022
Course: MBA ZG583 - Management of AI Products

1. Purpose
We used Artificial Intelligence as a collaboration and productivity tool during the Product Management activities for IdeaHub AI.
Artificial Intelligence helped us explore alternatives, structure Product Management artefacts, challenge assumptions and accelerate content creation.
However, the final product decisions were made by us.
We did not treat Artificial Intelligence-generated content as automatically correct. Suggestions were reviewed, refined or rejected based on:
• course concepts;
• product scope;
• faculty feedback;
• our professional experience; and
• consistency with the IdeaHub AI product vision.

2. AI Tool Used
Microsoft Copilot
Microsoft Copilot was the primary Artificial Intelligence assistant used during the assignment.
It was used to support:
• topic exploration;
• product positioning;
• Product Management artefact creation;
• alternative analysis;
• scope refinement;
• user journey design;
• risk identification;
• evaluation thinking; and
• documentation.

3. Where AI Helped
3.1 Topic Exploration
Artificial Intelligence helped us compare potential capstone ideas based on:
• relevance to the course;
• availability of a clear business problem;
• presence of repeatable decisions;
• suitability for Artificial Intelligence;
• ability to define a Minimum Viable Product; and
• alignment with our professional experience.
The alternatives considered included transformation opportunity assessment, process redesign, enterprise agent governance and other automation-related concepts.
We selected IdeaHub AI because opportunity discovery, qualification and prioritisation provided a clear enterprise workflow and strong alignment with the course.

3.2 Product Scope Refinement
The initial idea covered broad enterprise automation opportunity identification.
Artificial Intelligence helped explore narrower product boundaries.
We refined the product towards:
Artificial Intelligence-assisted transformation opportunity discovery, qualification and prioritisation.

Following faculty feedback, Process Mining was incorporated as an additional source of opportunity discovery.
We deliberately decided not to turn IdeaHub AI into a full Process Mining product.
Instead, Process Mining findings are treated as inputs that require further business qualification.

3.3 Product Management Artefacts
Artificial Intelligence assisted us in structuring and drafting Product Management artefacts such as:
• product strategy;
• Jobs to Be Done;
• Product Requirements Document;
• feature prioritisation;
• Minimum Viable Product definition;
• roadmap;
• success metrics;
• Human-in-the-Loop design;
• data and model strategy;
• business and scaling considerations; and
• Responsible Artificial Intelligence risks.
The generated content was reviewed against the approved product scope before being retained.

3.4 Alternative Evaluation
Artificial Intelligence was useful for generating alternative product approaches and challenging whether Artificial Intelligence was required for every capability.
This helped reinforce the product principle that IdeaHub AI should be capable of recommending:
• process redesign;
• deterministic business rules;
• workflow automation;
• Robotic Process Automation;
• Predictive Machine Learning;
• Generative Artificial Intelligence;
• Agentic Artificial Intelligence; or
• a hybrid approach.
This prevented the product concept from assuming that Artificial Intelligence is always the appropriate transformation solution.

3.5 User Experience and Trust Design
Artificial Intelligence helped generate alternative ways of presenting:
• missing information;
• evidence confidence;
• assumptions;
• intervention recommendations;
• risks; and
• human review options.
We retained the principle that the user should be able to understand and challenge the recommendation without needing to understand the internal operation of the underlying Artificial Intelligence model.

3.6 Risk Identification
Artificial Intelligence assisted in identifying possible risks.
The most relevant risks were then selected and adapted specifically to IdeaHub AI.
Examples include:
• hallucinated business facts;
• persuasive-description bias;
• unnecessary technology force-fit;
• automation bias;
• false precision;
• confidential enterprise information exposure;
• Process Mining misinterpretation; and
• insufficient human oversight.

4. Where AI Output Required Human Correction
Artificial Intelligence accelerated the work but did not always produce the right product decision.
The following examples demonstrate where human judgement changed the direction.

Example 1 - Product Scope Was Initially Too Broad
Artificial Intelligence initially suggested broader concepts covering:
• enterprise-wide opportunity discovery;
• portfolio optimisation;
• Process Mining;
• prioritisation; and
• transformation planning.
We considered this scope too wide for the first product release.
We therefore narrowed the first product wedge to qualification of an individual transformation opportunity.
Broader discovery and portfolio capabilities were moved into later product releases.

Example 2 - Process Mining Could Have Become a Separate Product
After Process Mining was introduced into the concept, Artificial Intelligence generated options where IdeaHub AI itself performed broader Process Mining activities.
We rejected this direction.
Process Mining platforms already specialise in process discovery and analysis.
We therefore positioned Process Mining as an input into IdeaHub AI, while IdeaHub AI focuses on translating process findings into transformation opportunities and assessing whether those opportunities deserve investment.

Example 3 - Avoiding Unsupported Quantification
Artificial Intelligence can generate plausible-looking benefit percentages, cost reductions or efficiency improvements even where there is no supporting evidence.
We deliberately excluded unsupported quantified benefits.
Where evidence is unavailable, IdeaHub AI is designed to:
• identify the information gap;
• record an assumption; or
• request clarification.

Example 4 - Avoiding Automatic AI Recommendations
Some early product alternatives emphasised Artificial Intelligence and Agentic Artificial Intelligence as the primary transformation outcomes.
We changed this approach.
IdeaHub AI should recommend the least complex intervention that adequately addresses the business problem.
This may mean recommending process redesign or conventional automation rather than Artificial Intelligence.

Example 5 - Human Accountability
Artificial Intelligence can generate and explain an assessment, but we decided that it should not make the final transformation investment decision.
Human reviewers therefore retain the ability to:
• accept;
• modify;
• request more information; or
• reject the recommendation.
Material overrides also retain a human rationale.

5. Human Contribution
Our role in the Artificial Intelligence-assisted workflow included:
• selecting the final project topic;
• accepting faculty feedback;
• defining product boundaries;
• selecting the primary target users;
• narrowing the first product wedge;
• deciding how Process Mining should be incorporated;
• deciding where Artificial Intelligence should and should not be used;
• reviewing Product Management artefacts;
• defining human decision rights;
• excluding unsupported claims;
• selecting product-specific risks; and
• approving the final product direction.
Artificial Intelligence accelerated ideation and artefact creation.
Product judgement remained human led.

6. Key Learning
Our main learning from using Artificial Intelligence during Product Management is that Artificial Intelligence is useful for quickly generating options and exposing considerations that may otherwise be missed.
However, broader and more sophisticated recommendations are not automatically better.
Human judgement remained especially important when deciding:
• product scope;
• customer value;
• intervention complexity;
• evidence quality;
• risk;
• governance; and
• what not to build.
The collaboration therefore reinforced the same principle used within IdeaHub AI itself:
Artificial Intelligence can improve decision support, but accountability for the decision should remain with the appropriate human.
